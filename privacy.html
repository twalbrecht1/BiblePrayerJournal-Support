Support Privacy

Last updated October 9, 2026

Privacy Policy

Your journal writing is saved on your device and, from version 1.1, syncs through your private iCloud account when available. Scripture imports connect separately to outside providers. Version 1.2’s shared import service is active; the differences from version 1.1 are described below.

Information you enter

The app stores the information you choose to enter, including profile names, activity preferences, Bible versions, journal entries, passage references, verse text, reflections, prayer requests and answers, dates, topics, favorites, and memory verse practice history. Prayer Pages also store their names, selected topics, display order, archive state, dated page notes, dated prayer updates, and recorded prayer times.

Local storage, iCloud sync, and profiles

Profiles and entries are stored locally so saved writing is available offline. From version 1.1, profiles, settings, topic catalogs, and journal entries—including reflections, prayers and answers, verse text and attribution, favorites, and memory practice metadata—and Prayer Pages, dated page notes, prayer updates, and recorded prayer times also synchronize through Apple’s CloudKit service in your private iCloud account when enabled. Apple stores and processes this data to provide synchronization between devices using the same Apple Account, under its privacy policy. The journal is not published to a public CloudKit database or sent to a developer-hosted journal server. The developer does not receive your profile names, reflections, or prayers through the app. You can control the app’s iCloud access in device Settings; disabling sync or signing out does not delete existing local writing. Profiles separate writing but are not password protected; anyone with access to the app on your unlocked device may access them.

Scripture imports

When you import a passage or load Verse of the Day, the selected passage reference and translation are sent to a Scripture provider. WEB and KJV use bible-api.com. Version 1.1 licensed imports connect directly to API.Bible using a personal key. Version 1.2 routes NIV, NLT, and NASB imports through the app’s service hosted on Cloudflare, using the app owner’s provider key. The service receives the passage reference, translation, optional edition ID, and device verification data. It sends Scripture requests to API.Bible and does not receive your profile names, reflections, prayer text, or topic selections. Cloudflare, Apple’s device-verification service, and Scripture providers handle connection information under their respective policies.

Device verification, API keys, and Scripture usage reports

Version 1.2 does not request, store, or use a personal API.Bible key. The app owner’s key is stored as an encrypted server secret. Apple App Attest verifies genuine copies of the app without a journal login. A device-specific key identifier is kept in that device’s Keychain; the server retains its public verification key and request counter, without a profile ID or journal writing. Challenges expire after two minutes. The service keeps rate-limit counters and daily salted IP hashes, removed after expiry; it does not store raw IP addresses or request bodies in its database. Cloudflare may separately process connection information. Older personal API keys saved by version 1.1 remain unused in the device Keychain and do not sync or appear in exports. Deleting their profile attempts to remove them on that device. When API.Bible content is displayed, the app sends the provider Scripture usage tokens with randomly generated installation and session identifiers, without your name or journal writing. See API.Bible’s usage reporting guidance.

Temporary licensed Scripture storage

In version 1.2, new imported NIV, NLT, and NASB entries save passage references, editions, copyright information, and your own writing in the journal. Provider text is cached separately on the device and hosted service, refreshed after 14 days and never displayed after 30 days without a refresh. Device cache files are excluded from backups and protected while the device is locked; expired files are removed when the app next opens or accesses the cache. Server cleanup removes expired text. Licensed cached text and usage tokens are not saved to new permanent journal records or synchronized through CloudKit. Original records are preserved on upgrade; editing an older managed entry adopts reference storage. Manually entered and public-domain Scripture retains its existing local and iCloud behavior.

Advertising and analytics

The current app does not include advertising or a separate general-purpose analytics service. The Scripture usage reports described above support the Bible provider’s content usage requirements. The app does not sell your journal information.

Exporting and sharing

If you export a journal or share an entry, you choose its destination using the system’s Files or sharing controls. An export contains the profile’s writing, topics, settings, entry metadata, Prayer Pages, dated notes, and prayer times, including archived pages and disabled areas, and excludes API keys. In version 1.2, imported licensed Scripture text is excluded from both exports and entry shares; its reference, version, and attribution remain included. Sharing an individual entry uses the entry’s writing and reference; its dated prayer history is included in the full journal export. Exported files and shared copies are handled by the locations and services you choose. Deleting an entry in the app does not delete copies you have exported or shared.

Support and this website

This support website is hosted on GitHub Pages and does not add analytics scripts, tracking cookies, or a contact form that collects your journal data. GitHub may process website request information under its privacy statement. Support and feedback submitted through GitHub Issues are processed by GitHub and are publicly visible. Do not include private journal writing, personal prayer requests, names, or API keys in a submission.

Retention and deletion

Prayer Pages and prayer history use the same local storage and private iCloud account as the profile’s journal. Prayers are not copied into separate pages; pages show prayers matching any of their selected topics. Archiving a page keeps its notes, and editing page topics keeps saved prayers and their history. Renaming or removing a topic updates page selections; page notes remain saved even if no topics remain selected. Deleting a prayer also removes that prayer’s dated updates and prayer times while keeping daily page notes. Deleting a profile removes its Prayer Pages and history along with its entries.

The Journal areas switches change visibility for the current profile. Turning Bible Journal, Prayer Journal, or Memory Verses off keeps its saved entries and history in local storage and iCloud, and includes them in journal exports. Re-enabling the area displays its saved writing, favorites, prayer answers, and memory practice history again. These area preferences sync with the profile’s existing settings.

Entries remain in local storage and, when synchronized, your private iCloud database until you delete them. Entry and profile deletions propagate to iCloud and other synced devices when connected. Deleting a profile removes its entries and attempts to remove the API key on the device where you perform the deletion; Keychain credentials on other devices may remain. Uninstalling the app, signing out of iCloud, or disabling sync does not delete writing already stored in iCloud. Delete unwanted profiles or entries while sync is available if you want those deletions to reach your other devices. Version 1.1 can explicitly remove a personal key in Settings → Bible Connection → Remove connection; version 1.2 removes that setup screen and no longer reads the key. Keychain credentials can remain after uninstalling an app, so remove a version 1.1 connection or delete its old profile before uninstalling if you want to remove those unused credentials as well. Keep a backup of writing you want to retain.

Policy changes and contact

This policy may be updated as the app’s features or data practices change. The latest revision date appears above. For questions, visit the support page or open a support request using a general description without private information.
